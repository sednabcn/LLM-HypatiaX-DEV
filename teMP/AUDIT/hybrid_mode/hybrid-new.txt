The one thing I'd change in your hypothesis
I would not say:
fixed RNG + 6–8 guesses doesn't perturb the RNG-driven trajectory enough
That's a little too strong.
A fixed seed guarantees the same random-number stream, but it does not imply the same evolutionary trajectory once the initial population differs.
The guesses change the initial state of the search. From there, the same deterministic sequence of random choices can operate on two different populations and therefore produce different mutations, selections, and descendants.
So the better causal statement is:
The two searches are deterministic conditional on their respective initialized populations; the guesses alter the initial population, but in these Nguyen problems that perturbation apparently does not alter the final selected solution.
That distinction matters.
And PySR 2.0.0a1 explicitly introduced equation guesses as a way to seed the evolutionary search, with a fraction_replaced_guesses control for how aggressively guesses replace the initial population. 
So there is no reason to expect "same seed" alone to make guessed and unseeded runs identical.
Why byte-identical answers are nevertheless plausible
What you're observing is actually quite compatible with a strong convergence basin:
                    same deterministic RNG
                           │
             ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
             │                           │
       cold initial pop             guessed initial pop
             │                           │
             └────── evolutionary search ┘
                         │
                  same excellent basin
                         │
                  same selected form
                         │
                same coefficient fit
In particular, if the LLM guesses are already close to the forms PySR independently discovers, they don't necessarily change the eventual winner. They can give the search a head start without changing the endpoint.
The striking part of your observation is not merely identical symbolic structure. It's the matching fitted constants to many significant digits. That makes the "same final basin / same deterministic refinement" explanation considerably more plausible than two unrelated stochastic searches merely happening to find equivalent formulas.
But it still isn't proof that the guesses had no effect. They could have:
    • changed the initial population, 
    • changed intermediate populations, 
    • caused an earlier discovery of the eventual winner, 
    • changed the hall-of-fame history, 
while leaving the final selected equation identical.
In fact, the current script records only the final expression/R²/time, so it cannot distinguish those possibilities.
