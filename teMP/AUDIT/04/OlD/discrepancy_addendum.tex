\begin{abstract}
While tracing and fixing the code responsible for Issue~4 of the original
audit (\emph{CI/SD Statistical Incompatibility}, \S10.1/Appendix~G), the
repair surfaced a chain of data-provenance problems that are \emph{not} code
bugs in the ordinary sense: several source-of-truth files in
\texttt{hypatiax/data/results/ablation/exp1\_ablation/} report materially
different values for the same underlying quantity, and at least one of
these files can be shown to have a genuine label/index misattribution bug
--- it is not merely stale, it is internally wrong. This addendum documents
each discrepancy with its supporting evidence, gives a best-available
reconciled table for Table~6 (LLM Ablation), and flags what still requires
a human decision before anything is republished. Numbering continues from
the original 13-issue audit as Issues~14--16.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Issue 14 --- Checkpoint-Key Collision in \texttt{exp1\_ablation.py} (Root Cause Confirmed, Fixed)}
\textbf{Category: [RESOLVED --- root cause found in source, fix applied and verified]}

\subsection*{Location}
\texttt{hypatiax/experiments/benchmarks/exp1\_ablation.py}, function
\texttt{run\_experiment()}, main sharded loop (originally
\texttt{for eq\_idx, eq in enumerate(\_CORE\_15\_RUN)}).

\subsection*{What was wrong}
The original diagnosis (below, preserved for the record) found that
\texttt{exp1\_rf01\_mannwhitney.json} pairs equation \emph{names} with far-$R^2$
\emph{values} that belong to different equations. Tracing this into the
actual source revealed the exact mechanism: the main experiment loop keyed
each equation's checkpoint entry by its \emph{position within the
shard-filtered list} (\texttt{eq\_idx} from \texttt{enumerate(\_CORE\_15\_RUN)}),
not by its true index in the full \texttt{CORE\_15} list. Every shard's
\texttt{eq\_idx} therefore restarts at 0, so two different shards' first
equations both write to checkpoint key \texttt{"0"}, their second equations
both write to key \texttt{"1"}, and so on. When shard checkpoints are
combined into one \texttt{all\_results} dict, the shard merged in later
silently overwrites the earlier shard's entries at every colliding key.

This was confirmed with an exact, non-coincidental 3-for-3 match: the
\texttt{"chemistry"} shard (\texttt{Arrhenius=local~0, Henderson-Hasselbalch=local~1,
Rate~Law=local~2}) collided with the \texttt{"defi\_amm"} shard
(\texttt{Impermanent~Loss=local~0, Price~Impact=local~1, Constant~Product=local~2}):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
\textbf{rf01's label} & \textbf{rf01's value} & \textbf{Real owner of that value} \\
\midrule
"Arrhenius" (local 0)              & $-8259.31$            & \textbf{Impermanent Loss} (local 0) \\
"Henderson-Hasselbalch" (local 1)  & $1.0$                 & \textbf{Price Impact} (local 1) \\
"Rate Law" (local 2)               & $-1.85\times10^{46}$  & \textbf{Constant Product} (local 2) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Notably, the codebase already contained the correct fix, unused:
\texttt{EQ\_ID~=~\{eq["name"]:~i~for~i,~eq~in~enumerate(CORE\_15)\}} exists (with a
comment explicitly calling it a \emph{"Stable ID map --- checkpoint key never
changes"}), but the main loop never called it, using the shard-local
\texttt{enumerate()} index instead.

\subsection*{Fix applied}
Changed the loop to iterate \texttt{\_CORE\_15\_RUN} directly and look up each
equation's true global index via the existing \texttt{EQ\_ID} map:
\begin{quote}
\ttfamily\small
eq\_idx = EQ\_ID[eq["name"]] \\
eq\_key = str(eq\_idx)
\end{quote}
Verified by simulation: reproducing the exact chemistry/defi\_amm shard
collision with the old logic recreates the corruption (chemistry's entries
vanish, overwritten); the same simulation with the fix applied preserves
all 15 equations under their correct, unique, collision-free keys.

\subsection*{Action still required}
All checkpoint files and \texttt{exp1\_rf01\_mannwhitney.json} produced
\emph{before} this fix were built from corrupted keys and cannot be
salvaged or partially repaired --- they must be deleted (not resumed
from) and \texttt{exp1\_ablation.py} re-run from a clean checkpoint state.
Resuming from an old checkpoint after this fix would re-introduce the
corruption for any equation already present under the wrong key.

\section*{Issue 15 --- Four Independent Mann-Whitney Computations, Four Different Answers}
\textbf{Category: [OPEN --- needs statistical re-analysis + author decision]}

\subsection*{Location}
\texttt{jmlr\_paper\_main.tex} (published claim) vs.\
\texttt{exp1\_rf01\_mannwhitney.json} vs.\
\texttt{\_analysis.json} (three internal tiers) vs.\
\texttt{exp1\_ablation\_run.log}, line 453.

\subsection*{What is wrong}
No two of the four following sources agree on sample size, test
statistic, or significance for the same nominal claim (``does HypatiaX
beat PySR-only on far-extrapolation $R^2$''):

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccl}
\toprule
\textbf{Source} & \textbf{$n$} & \textbf{$U$} & \textbf{$p$ (two-sided)} & \textbf{Significant?} & \textbf{Note} \\
\midrule
Paper (\S10.1, current)         & 15 & 0     & $1.11\times10^{-6}$ & Yes & Not reproduced by any raw file found \\
\texttt{rf01\_mannwhitney.json} & 3  & 2.5   & 0.5066               & No  & Values internally mislabeled (Issue~14) \\
\texttt{\_analysis.json} (all-$n$)     & 10 & 44.0 & 0.6735         & No  & \\
\texttt{\_analysis.json} (excl.\ fail) & 10 & 44.0 & 0.6735         & No  & Identical to all-$n$ tier \\
\texttt{\_analysis.json} (success subset) & 6 & 20.0 & 0.7976       & No  & Flagged by the file itself, see below \\
\texttt{exp1\_ablation\_run.log} line 453 & 2\footnote{Log reports ``RF-01 pairs: 2'', skipping 13 equations including Henderson-Hasselbalch, which \texttt{rf01\_mannwhitney.json} instead treats as a valid, included pair (giving it $n=3$). The log and the JSON file produced on the same run day disagree with each other about which equations were even eligible.} & --- & --- & --- & Skip list disagrees with \texttt{rf01\_mannwhitney.json}'s own skip list \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every raw-data source that could be located agrees on one thing: the
result is \textbf{not statistically significant} at $\alpha=0.05$. None
reproduces the paper's current $U=0$, $p=1.11\times10^{-6}$ claim in any
form, at any sample size.

Most notably, \texttt{\_analysis.json} contains a self-generated warning
that directly addresses this:
\begin{quote}
\small\ttfamily
"WARN\_MW\_SUCCESS\_NOT\_SIGNIFICANT: Tier-3 (success-subset) Mann-Whitney
one-sided p=0.3988 (n=6) --- not significant at $\alpha$=0.05. Primary
paper claim (\S10.7) may be weaker than expected. Investigate."
\end{quote}
This is the pipeline's own analysis code flagging, at generation time,
that the number intended for publication does not hold up --- and,
per the log, that flag appears to have gone unactioned.

\subsection*{Fix required}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Fix Issue~14 first (the mislabeling bug) --- \texttt{rf01\_mannwhitney.json}
    cannot be trusted as-is.
  \item Determine which of the two remaining sample-size conventions is the
    intended one for \S10.1/\S10.7: the ``all equations, $n=10$'' tier or
    the ``success subset, $n=6$'' tier in \texttt{\_analysis.json}. Both are
    non-significant, so this doesn't change the qualitative conclusion, but
    it changes which $U$/$p$ pair is quoted.
  \item \S10.1 and \S10.7's prose (and the abstract, if it makes the same
    claim) need to be rewritten to state the result is \textbf{not}
    statistically significant, with the corrected $U$/$p$/$n$ and,
    ideally, the rank-biserial effect size ($r=0.12$ for the all-$n$ tier)
    for context. This is a substantive scientific-conclusion change, not a
    number swap, and needs author sign-off before merging --- it is
    explicitly out of scope for this pipeline fix to make unilaterally.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Issue 16 --- Wrong Key Name in \texttt{run\_analysis.py} (Root Cause Confirmed, Fixed)}
\textbf{Category: [RESOLVED --- root cause found in source, fix applied and verified]}

\subsection*{Location}
\texttt{run\_analysis.py}, line building \texttt{eq\_name} inside the main
per-record loop that constructs \texttt{instability\_rows} and
\texttt{skipped\_equations}.

\subsection*{What was wrong}
\begin{quote}
\ttfamily\small
eq\_name = r.get("equation\_name", r.get("equation\_id", "?"))
\end{quote}
The real per-equation record schema (confirmed directly from
\texttt{\_merged.json}) uses the keys \texttt{"name"} and \texttt{"task\_id"}.
Neither \texttt{"equation\_name"} nor \texttt{"equation\_id"} exists anywhere
in the real data, so this line unconditionally fell through to the
\texttt{"?"} placeholder for every single row, regardless of input. This is
not a data-availability problem --- the real name was present in every
record the whole time, just under a different key than the code checked
for.

\subsection*{Fix applied}
\begin{quote}
\ttfamily\small
eq\_name = r.get("name", r.get("task\_id", "?"))
\end{quote}
Verified directly against the real \texttt{\_merged.json}: the old logic
returns \texttt{"?"} for all 15 records; the new logic correctly resolves
\texttt{"Allometric Scaling"}, \texttt{"Arrhenius"}, \texttt{"Constant
Product"}, etc.

\subsection*{Action still required}
Re-run the analysis step (\texttt{run\_analysis.py}) once fresh,
non-corrupted \texttt{\_merged.json} input is available from the Issue~14
fix, to produce a \texttt{\_analysis.json} with both correct names
\emph{and} correct values.

\section*{Verification --- End-to-End Integration Test}
\textbf{Category: [VERIFIED --- all three fixes tested together, exit code 0]}

Because Issues~14 and~16 sit upstream of \texttt{generate\_tables.py} (whose
own fixes were verified in isolation, against individually-uploaded real
files, in earlier sessions), the three fixes had not previously been run
\emph{together} as one pipeline. A synthetic-but-schema-accurate
integration test was constructed to close that gap:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Simulated the fixed \texttt{exp1\_ablation.py} logic} across
    five shards (chemistry, biology, physics, defi\_amm, defi\_risk), each
    equation given a unique, distinguishable fake value. Asserted all 15
    equations survive the merge under their correct global key with no
    collisions --- \textbf{PASS}.
  \item \textbf{Simulated the fixed \texttt{run\_analysis.py} logic}
    (\texttt{r.get("name", r.get("task\_id", "?"))}) against the merged
    output from step~1. Asserted zero \texttt{"?"} placeholders across all
    15 rows, and that a synthetic \texttt{rf01\_mannwhitney.json} built from
    the same corrected source now matches \texttt{\_merged.json} by name to
    full precision (i.e.\ Issue~14's original symptom cannot recur once the
    key collision is fixed upstream) --- \textbf{PASS}.
  \item \textbf{Fed the resulting files through the actual, unmodified
    \texttt{generate\_tables.py}} (not a simulation --- the real script) via
    its normal \texttt{--experiment exp1\_ablation} entry point. The run
    exited \textbf{code 0}, all three expected tables generated, the
    pre-flight JSON audit correctly reported the file as found (no
    fallback triggered), and the Mann-Whitney tablenote rendered correctly
    from the synthetic-but-now-internally-consistent data.
\end{enumerate}

This confirms the three fixes are compatible with each other and with the
existing pipeline plumbing. It does \emph{not} replace re-running the real
experiment: the synthetic data here is schema-accurate but not a
substitute for real PySR/HypatiaX output, and the actual significance
question (Issue~15) can only be answered once \texttt{exp1\_ablation.py} is
re-run for real, from a clean checkpoint state, using the fixed code.
